What the app actually looks like

Eight screens, drawn from the decisions already made in PLAN.md. The point of this page is not the styling — it is to show what is on screen at the moment a dose is taken, and what is deliberately absent.

Not code, and not approved. Every number here is a worked example from the plan — a 50 g meal at a reading of 330 gives 11 units. Nothing on this page has been reviewed by either reviewer, and §20.1's two go-aheads still gate any implementation.

The dose, and its working

One integer, larger than anything else on screen. Beneath it, the two parts it came from — so an error in either input is visible without going back.

No trailing zeros, no bare decimal points. “11”, never “11.0”; “0.5”, never “.5”. Both are documented dosing-error sources.

No number at all

Below 70 the app shows no dose, no working, no partial figure it could be talked into. Nothing else renders on this screen — no ketone card, no timing line, nothing to scroll past.

This gate needs none of his ratios to be correct, which makes it the most valuable thing in the app on day one.

The inputs, not the dose

At 20 units and above the app withholds the number and shows what you typed instead. Once a dose is on screen you stop looking at the inputs — so the pause has to come first.

250 g of carbohydrate is a plate weight typed into a carbohydrate field. It would have produced 31 units.

The same warning, second time today

His readings sit above 250 most days, so the ketone card would appear at nearly every meal — and a warning that always fires stops being read.

So the card shrinks to a line after the first time each day. The instruction never changes and it never collapses behind “more”. Compare it with the full version two screens up: same words, less furniture.

Show as text to screenshot

Sentences, not abbreviations

No “ISF”, no “ICR”, no “bolus”. Each setting is a sentence a person can check against what their doctor told them, which is the only way a wrong ratio ever gets spotted.

The Lantus dose is recorded and visually separated, because a second number labelled “units” next to a calculated dose is exactly the confusion worth avoiding.

This is not a medical device

It has no regulatory clearance and has not been clinically validated. Check every dose yourself. It is set up for one specific person's prescription. Neither the meter manufacturer nor the insulin manufacturer endorses it.

If the number looks nothing like what you usually take

Do not assume either number is the right one. Neither this app nor your usual dose has been checked against the other. Show your doctor the exported record and let them decide.

The disagreement, said once, up front

The app will say 11 where he currently injects 25. That gap is the reason the project exists, and the wording deliberately backs neither number.

It appears at setup and then lives in the export — never as a line beside a calculated dose, where it would become an anchor pointing at the dose that caused his lows.

What these screens are avoiding

design constraints

Each of these is a specific failure the layout is built to prevent, not a general principle.

The trapWhat the screens do about it
Two numbers both labelled “units” The calculated dose is the only large number on a result screen. Injected appears solely in history, in its own column. The Lantus dose lives in settings behind different styling and never on a result screen.
A warning that fires every time Band E de-escalates from a card to a line after its first appearance each day — but the words never change and it is never hidden behind “more”.
A dose seen before its inputs At 20 units and up the number is withheld and the inputs are shown instead. There is no partial figure and no preview on that screen.
Numerals that misread Every figure is IBM Plex Mono with tabular numerals — 1 and 7 are unambiguous, columns align. 11 never 11.0; 0.5 never .5.
A low blood sugar screen you can scroll past Below 70, nothing else renders. No ketone card, no timing line, no dose — the treat-first instruction is the entire screen.
Plate weight typed as carbohydrate The field says “carbohydrate in this meal”, the empty screen carries the biryani example, and a 250 g entry alone reaches 25 units — which trips the confirmation at every blood sugar level.
