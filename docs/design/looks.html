Three interfaces · not three palettes

The same job, built three different ways

Same two inputs, same arithmetic, same refusal at 65. What changes is the structure of the interface itself — what you see first, how you enter a number, whether state lives in a card or in the whole screen.

1 · Card

the one you have

Everything on one screen, in ranked order. Inputs, dose, working, warnings — a single scroll, nothing hidden. Warnings are cards with a severity stripe; the dose is the largest thing but shares the page.

Costs: it looks like software from a clinic. And on a busy screen the dose competes with everything around it.

2 · Band

state is the whole surface

You see where the reading sits before you read the number. The top of every screen is tinted by the band — green at target, amber high, red low — and a gradient scale shows the reading's position against 70, 150 and 250. The dose lives on its own dark panel so it never competes. Actions sit in a thumb-zone sheet at the bottom. The refusal takes over the entire screen rather than appearing as a card on it.

Costs: colour is doing real work, so it must survive colour-blindness and sunlight — the number and the words have to carry the same meaning alone. And the gradient is decoration the moment it stops being accurate.

3 · Step

one thing per screen

No form at all. One question per screen, its own keypad, progress along the top — so nothing else is on screen while a number is being typed. Dark ground because this gets used before dawn and after dark. The dose arrives as its own screen, at the largest size of the three, with the working folded into a panel beneath it.

Costs: more taps for something done three times a day, and a custom keypad means rebuilding what the phone already does well. The style is also the most fashionable of the three, which is the one that ages.
